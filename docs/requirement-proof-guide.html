How To Present The Demo

  • Follow the order in this guide. Do not jump to the next module before the current one shows a visible result.
  • For every requirement, show three things: the page, the action, and the saved result.
  • When a feature writes data, open the list view, detail view, log view, or notification row right after saving.
  • When the evaluator asks for proof, show the exact URL, the form submission, and the stored record or log entry.
  • Use the alias routes in this guide if you want the shortest demo path.

Step 1. Show The App Is Ready

  1. Open the project in terminal and confirm the app is seeded: php artisan migrate --seed.
  2. Build the frontend once: npm run build.
  3. Start the server: php artisan serve --host=127.0.0.1 --port=8001.
  4. Open http://127.0.0.1:8001 in the browser and show the login page.
  5. Explain that the demo uses seeded roles and alias accounts for proof.

Step 2. Prove Authentication And Role-Based Access

Admin

Log in as admin@example.com or admin@plexus.test.

Open /dashboard and /admin.

Show the admin dashboard, menu, monitoring cards, and the admin-only pages.

Supplier

Log in as supplier@example.com or supplier@plexus.test.

Open /supplier/products or /commerce/products.

Show supplier-only product management and order-related screens.

Buyer

Log in as buyer@example.com or buyer@plexus.test.

Open /products, /cart, and /buyer/tickets.

Show the buyer path for shopping and support.

Marketing Manager

Log in as marketing@example.com or marketing@plexus.test.

Open /marketing/campaigns, /social/posts, and /workflow/rules.

Show marketing, social, and workflow permissions.

Step 3. Prove E-Commerce

  1. As a buyer, open the Marketplace Catalog entry in the sidebar or go directly to /products, then show at least one seeded product card, for example Commercial Water Pump 100L, Industrial Dock Plate 1500lb, or Wholesale Filing Cabinet Pro.
  2. Open the product detail page from any card and point to the visible MOQ, Stock, SKU, Category, current price, and the tier table.
  3. Open /products/bulk-orders and /products/moq-pricing to show the same underlying catalog data through two different routes: one focused on bulk quantities, the other on MOQ pricing.
  4. Switch to admin and open /admin/products. Create or edit a product to show Supplier, SKU, Product Name, Description, Regular Price, MOQ, Bulk Price, Stock Quantity, and Status.
  5. Open /admin/bulk-pricing for the same product and show the separate MOQ control and tier price table. Explain that basic product details and quantity-based pricing are managed in two different workspaces.
  6. Save the product or pricing change and immediately return to the list or product detail view so the stored values are visible.
  7. Switch back to buyer, add the seeded product to cart using at least the MOQ quantity, then open /cart and show the item count, unit price, tier price if applied, and line total.
  8. Continue to /checkout, complete the checkout flow, and show /checkout/success/{orderNumber}. If the payment gateway is not fully configured, the page may say Checkout received, but the order is still created.
  9. Open /commerce/orders and /invoices, then open the invoice detail page. Show the generated order number, invoice number, order total, item lines, and download/preview actions.
  10. If the orders page was empty before checkout, explain that this is expected. The proof is the new row that appears after the order is placed and the invoice is issued.

Step 4. Prove Supplier Onboarding

  1. Open /register-supplier while logged out.
  2. Fill in the supplier form and submit it.
  3. Show the saved submission, then log in as admin.
  4. Open /admin/suppliers or the admin supplier review path and show the pending request.
  5. Approve or reject the supplier request and show the status change.

Step 5. Prove CRM

  1. Log in as admin or marketing manager.
  2. Open /crm/customers, /crm/purchases, /crm/segments, and /crm/interactions.
  3. Open /admin/leads and then /admin/leads/create.
  4. Create a lead, save it, then open the lead list to show the new row.
  5. Edit the lead and show the updated data on the edit page and list page.
  6. Delete a lead if the evaluator wants CRUD proof, and show that the row disappears.
  7. Show a customer detail page with purchase history to prove the CRM is connected to commerce data.

Step 6. Prove Marketing

  1. Open /admin/campaigns or /marketing/campaigns.
  2. Open /marketing/campaigns/create and create a campaign.
  3. Open /marketing/templates and show template CRUD or selection.
  4. Save the campaign and return to the list page so the stored row is visible.
  5. Explain or demonstrate the scheduled send command: php artisan campaigns:send-scheduled.
  6. If there is a sent log, message log, or recipient log, show that as the proof artifact.

Step 7. Prove Social Automation

  1. Open /social/campaigns or /social/posts to show the basic social campaign list and the scheduled post workspace.
  2. Open /social/accounts or /social/accounts/create to show the publishing registry add/edit flow. Point out the Facebook Page ID, access token, and the separate account permission.
  3. Open /admin/social-posts or /social/posts to show the scheduled post list.
  4. Open /social/posts/create and create a scheduled Facebook or Instagram post tied to a specific account.
  5. Save it, then open /social/calendar to show the scheduled item on the calendar.
  6. Edit the post and show that the target platform, account, caption, campaign, and schedule persist.
  7. Delete one post if CRUD proof is needed.
  8. Explain the publish command: php artisan social-posts:publish-due, and mention that the scheduler can invoke it automatically on the server.

Step 8. Prove Workflow Automation

  1. Open /admin/automation-rules or /workflow/rules.
  2. Open /workflow/rules/create and create an IF / THEN rule, such as order.placed with send_email and send_sms.
  3. Save the rule and reopen the rules list to show it is stored.
  4. Trigger the relevant event, such as order placement, RFQ creation, or support ticket creation; RFQ creation is dispatched through the RfqCreated event listener.
  5. Open /workflow/logs and show the execution record.
  6. Point out the full JSON payload and result columns, the status pill, the error text, and the executed timestamp.
  7. If the evaluator asks for queue support, explain the worker command: php artisan queue:work.
  8. If the evaluator asks for cron support, run or explain php artisan workflow:close-stale-runs and show that php artisan schedule:work runs it automatically on the server.

Step 9. Prove Support And Notifications

  1. Open /buyer/tickets or /support/tickets.
  2. Create a support ticket, then show the ticket detail page.
  3. Reply to the ticket and show the reply thread.
  4. Update the ticket status or assignment if the role allows it.
  5. Show the notification entry or in-app message that was produced by the support flow.
  6. Explain that the system also supports supplier notifications and FAQ matching.

Step 10. Prove Admin, Settings, And RBAC

  1. Open /admin/users and show user management.
  2. Open /admin/modules and /settings/modules to prove module control.
  3. Show that locked modules such as admin and settings cannot be disabled.
  4. Open /admin/audit-logs if you need proof of admin-level changes.
  5. Demonstrate that different roles do not see the same menus or routes.
  6. If the evaluator asks for the customer profile alias, open /customer/profile.

Step 11. Prove API, Scheduler, Queue, And Backup

  1. Run php artisan route:list --path=api/v1 and show the API surface.
  2. Point out the key endpoints for products, orders, customers, campaigns, social posts, workflow logs, and support tickets.
  3. Run php artisan schedule:list and php artisan schedule:run if needed.
  4. Show the queue worker command: php artisan queue:work.
  5. Show the operational commands from docs/operations.html.
  6. Open database/backups/plexusbiz_assignment_backup.sql and mention that the database backup already exists for proof.

Short Viva Script

Opening

"I will prove the full assignment flow in order: login, e-commerce, CRM, marketing, social, workflow, support, admin settings, API, scheduler, and backup."

Evidence Rule

"For every feature, I will show the page, perform the action, and then open the saved record or log row."

Automation Rule

"For every automation, I will show the trigger, the execution result, and the workflow log with the full JSON payload."

Closeout

"The app is not placeholder-only. The required flows are saved, linked, and backed by logs, routes, and backup output."

What Counts As Final Proof

  • A visible page or route that matches the requirement.
  • A save action that creates or updates real data.
  • A list page, detail page, log row, or notification that shows the saved result.
  • A scheduler or queue command when the requirement depends on background work.
  • A backup file or operations document when the evaluator asks for deployment proof.
